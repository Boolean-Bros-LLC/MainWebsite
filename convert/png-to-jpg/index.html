    What this does

    Every PNG you drop is decoded by your browser, drawn onto a canvas at its original pixel dimensions, filled onto the background colour you choose, and re-encoded as a baseline JPEG. The file you download is a normal .jpg that opens anywhere — email clients, Word, government portals, print shops that refuse PNG.

    This is a converter, not a compressor. If you need a specific file size, use the image compressor, which searches for the highest quality that fits under your target.

    Why transparency needs a colour

    JPEG has no alpha channel. A logo with a transparent background has to be flattened onto something, so this tool fills those pixels with a solid colour. White is right for documents and forms; pick black or a custom colour if the image will sit on a dark background.

    Why the JPG is sometimes bigger

    PNG is excellent at flat colour — screenshots, diagrams and UI mockups compress beautifully. A photographic PNG will usually shrink when converted to JPEG; a screenshot of solid colours often will not. Lower the quality slider, or set a max dimension if the image is larger than you need.

    • JPG to PDF — combine images into one document.

    Questions

    Are my images uploaded?

    No. There is no server involved. Load the page, disconnect from the internet, and the converter still works — that is the test.

    Does it keep the resolution?

    Yes, width and height are preserved unless you set a maximum dimension. JPEG is lossy, so fine detail softens slightly, but the pixel dimensions do not change.

    Can I convert many files at once?

    Yes. Drop as many as you like and use “Download all”. Big batches take a moment because everything runs on your machine, not a server.

    Does it strip metadata?

    Yes. Re-encoding through a canvas drops EXIF, including GPS coordinates. That is usually what you want before posting an image publicly.
